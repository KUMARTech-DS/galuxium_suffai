How SuffAI reads garment images

Feature extraction from images: what we extract, how it works, and where it is used
Audience: business and commercial teams · Prepared October 2026

1. The short version

Every garment photo that enters SuffAI, whether it is a customer's item photographed during a laundry order or a product image uploaded by a brand, is turned into two things by Google's AI models:

A "label card" (readable attributes)

Plain facts about the garment such as type, colour, pattern, material, size and brand. These are read from the photo and from any care or brand labels visible in it.

type: shirt
colour: navy blue
pattern: checked
material: cotton
size: L
brand: unknown

A "visual fingerprint" (embedding)

A long list of numbers that captures how the garment looks overall: cut, texture, colour mix and style. Two garments that look alike have similar fingerprints, even if their label cards differ.

Lets us answer "how similar is this brand's shirt to what customers in this PIN code already wear?" as a number between 0 and 1.

Why both? The label card is precise but limited to the fields we ask for. The fingerprint captures overall look and style that words cannot. SuffAI combines them when ranking brand products for an audience.

3. How it works

Images enter through two doors. The extraction step is the same for both; what happens afterwards differs.

Door A: Customer garments (from laundry orders)

1Photo storedGarment photo is saved securely in Google Cloud Storage, tagged with the customer and order.
→
2Label cardGemini AI reads type, colour, pattern, material, size and brand, with a confidence for each.
→
3FingerprintGoogle's multimodal model creates the visual fingerprint.
→
4Same garment?Compared with the customer's wardrobe, then with all known garments. 92% or higher similarity counts as the same item.
→
5Wardrobe updatedMatched items get their PIN-code count increased; new items are added as a new unique garment.

Over time this builds a de-duplicated digital wardrobe for each customer and a picture of which garments are worn, and how often, in each PIN code, without counting the same shirt twice when it is washed again.

Door B: Brand products (Ads Lab campaigns)

1Brand uploads SKUsBrand selects target PIN codes and audience (age, gender, RFM tier) and uploads product images.
→
2Label cardSame Gemini extraction. Brand name is forced to the uploading brand.
→
3FingerprintSame multimodal model, so brand and customer garments are directly comparable.
→
4Compare with PINsFingerprint is compared with garments seen in each chosen PIN code; the top 5 closest are kept per PIN.
→
5RankVisual match, local popularity and attribute fit are blended into a 0 to 100 score.
Brand images never become customer data. Brand product images are compared read-only. They are never added to any customer's wardrobe or to PIN-code counts.

Quality checks built into extraction

  • Confidence gate: if every key field is 90% confident or more, the result is accepted automatically; otherwise it is marked Needs review. A detected brand with low confidence always goes to review.
  • Labels beat guesses: material, size and brand come from printed labels when readable. Visual guesses are flagged as such and given lower confidence.
  • Ranking still completes: if extraction or comparison fails for one product, the product is still scored on the parts that worked and is marked as failed, so one bad image does not stop the campaign.
  • Safe retries: each image is processed once even if the cloud delivers the notification twice.

4. How a brand product is ranked

For each uploaded product, the Ads Lab combines three signals into one score out of 100:

Rank score = 100 × ( 55% Visual match + 15% Local popularity + 30% Attribute fit )
SignalWeightWhat it meansComes from
Visual match55%How closely the product's look resembles the garments customers in the chosen PIN codes already own. PINs with more observed garments count for more.Visual fingerprint
Local popularity15%How much of the PIN's observed wardrobe is made up of the product's closest look-alikes, i.e. whether those similar garments are common or rare locally.Fingerprint + PIN counts
Attribute fit30%How well the product's label card matches the selected audience's known preferences for colour, pattern, garment type, material, size and brand.Label card + audience preferences

If a field is unknown for a product (for example size was not readable), that field is left out rather than counted against the product. If the audience filter is too narrow to have preference data, it relaxes to the PIN codes alone.

6. Privacy and trust

  • The AI sees garment images only. It is told never to infer a person's gender, age or income from them.
  • Brand users see aggregated, PIN-level insight only. Individual customers are not identifiable, and competitor brands are masked.
  • Brand catalogue images are compared read-only and never mixed into customer data.
  • Low-confidence results are flagged for review rather than silently trusted.

7. Glossary

Feature extractionTurning an image into structured information a system can use: here, a label card and a visual fingerprint.
Label card / attributesHuman-readable facts about a garment (type, colour, pattern, material, size, brand).
Visual fingerprint / embeddingA list of 1,408 numbers describing a garment's overall look. Similar-looking garments have similar fingerprints.
SimilarityA 0 to 1 score of how alike two fingerprints are (1 = identical look).
ConfidenceThe model's own 0 to 1 estimate of how sure it is about each attribute.
SKUA single product in a brand's catalogue.
RFM tierCustomer segment based on how Recently, how Frequently and how Much they spend.
